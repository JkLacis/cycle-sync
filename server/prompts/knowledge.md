## Cycle basics
- A cycle has four phases: Menstrual, Follicular, Ovulatory, Luteal. Day 1 is the first day of a period.
- Cycles commonly run every 21 to 35 days (about 28 on average); a period usually lasts 2 to 7 days, often about 5 (NHS).
- Ovulation usually happens about 10 to 16 days before the next period and is hard to pinpoint (NHS). The app estimates it at cycle length minus 14, so every phase date in the app is an estimate. Stress, illness and travel can shift the pattern. Many hormonal contraceptives stop ovulation, so the natural phase pattern may not apply; if she uses one, say phase-based ideas may matter less and don't assume phases.
- Typical phase lengths (In the FLO): Menstrual 3–7 days, Follicular 7–10, Ovulatory 3–4, Luteal 10–14.

## The four phases (POWR labels)
Suggestions below are inspired by *In the FLO* (Alisa Vitti) and written in our own words. Treat them as ideas to try.

**Follicular — Prepare.** Estrogen is rising; energy and curiosity tend to rise.
- Work: start new projects, brainstorm, tackle hard problems, research, seek new clients, plan the month ahead.
- Move: lighter, playful cardio — dance, jumping rope or rebounding, indoor cycling, hiking, some HIIT.
- Eat ideas: oats, barley, broccoli, carrots, zucchini, green peas, lentils, avocado, citrus, eggs, chicken, fermented foods like sauerkraut.
- Watch-outs: routine admin, over-booking, late nights.

**Ovulatory — Open Up.** Estrogen is at its highest; many feel most social and confident.
- Work: important conversations, pitching, negotiating, presenting, asking for a raise, job interviews, networking, sharing work publicly, giving talks.
- Move: highest-intensity options — HIIT, kettlebells, boot camp, kickboxing, indoor cycling, power yoga.
- Eat ideas: quinoa, corn, spinach, red peppers, asparagus, tomatoes, chard, berries, figs, apricots, red lentils, almonds, salmon, shrimp.
- Watch-outs: admin-heavy days, high stress, energy dips after big social days.

**Luteal — Work.** Progesterone is at its highest; energy gradually winds down across the phase.
- Work: deep focused work, admin and paperwork, reviewing documents, contracts and reports, wrapping up projects, helping a team hit deadlines, organising.
- Move: strength first, then gentler — weight lifting, Pilates, barre, yoga; shorter HIIT early in the phase.
- Eat ideas: steady, grounding meals with complex carbs — brown rice, millet, sweet potato, squash, cauliflower, cabbage, leafy greens, chickpeas, apples, pears, walnuts, turkey or cod.
- Watch-outs: starting big new pitches late in the phase, high stress, skipping meals or carbs.

**Menstrual — Rest.** All hormone levels are low; energy is often lowest.
- Work: review the past month, notice patterns, reassess goals, trust your gut on decisions, take frequent breaks, take a personal day if possible.
- Move: rest or gentle movement — walking, yin yoga, gentle mat Pilates.
- Eat ideas: warm, nourishing food — soups and stews, buckwheat, wild rice, beets, kale, mushrooms, seaweed (for example in miso soup), blueberries, kidney or adzuki beans; herbal tea.
- Watch-outs: big meetings, intense workouts, packed days.

## Planning with the cycle (In the FLO, paraphrased)
1. At the end of the month, look ahead at what's due.
2. Map the phases and place flexible projects where they fit.
3. Keep fixed deadlines where they fall.
4. If a task lands in a tougher phase, drop something non-essential that day and add one small self-care moment.
5. In meetings, bring the strength of the current phase.
6. Share the idea with the team.
Common energy leaks: not enough rest, skipping movement, skipping meals, saying yes when you mean no, not asking for help, money worries. Rest in the luteal and menstrual phases is framed as useful, not lazy.

## How the app works
- Alignment score (today only): each of today's tasks gets a level for the current phase — High Sync 100 (suits this phase), Good 75 (fine in any phase), Moderate 60 (better in another phase), Low Sync 0 (best kept for another phase). The score is the average; no tasks means no score. Below 50 the home screen switches to a softer "recovery" look with a "lighter day" suggestion.
- Task types and the phase they suit: pitch/presentation and networking/key conversation → Ovulatory; brainstorm/new project and planning/research → Follicular; deep work/admin, wrap-up and strength/Pilates/yoga → Luteal; review/reflection and rest/gentle walk → Menstrual; high-intensity workout → Follicular or Ovulatory; meetings suit any phase. Low Sync mostly means pitches, networking, meetings or intense workouts in the Menstrual phase, brainstorming in Luteal, wrapping up in Follicular, review work in Ovulatory. The "avoid" pairings are the app's own inference; the book lists strengths, not prohibitions.
- Check-ins (Track Today): energy 0–10, mood (Low/Okay/Good/Great), focus (Low/Medium/High), sleep (<8h / 8h / >8h). The app shows values only; it doesn't interpret them medically.
- Users change tasks on the Alignment screen, cycle data in Settings → Cycle settings, check-ins in Cycle Plan → Track Today.

## Evidence (be honest about it)
- Exercise: a 2020 meta-analysis (McNulty et al., Sports Medicine) found only a trivial drop in exercise performance in the early follicular phase and advised individual approaches rather than general phase-based rules. A 2023 umbrella review (Colenso-Semple et al.) found no influence of cycle phase on strength performance or resistance-training gains.
- Thinking and work: a 2025 meta-analysis (Jang et al., PLoS One) found no robust changes in cognitive performance across the cycle.
- Food: there is no good evidence that phase-specific foods change hormones or symptoms; the food ideas are about variety and enjoyment.
- So: phase-based suggestions can help with planning and self-awareness, and many people notice their own patterns (check-ins help with this), but they are not proven performance rules. Individual experience matters more than the phase label.

## Never recommend
- Calorie restriction, calorie targets, intermittent fasting, keto/paleo or other restrictive plans tied to phases, or anything framed around weight loss.
- "Adrenal fatigue" (not a recognised diagnosis — Endocrine Society, Mayo Clinic), claims that cycle syncing cures PMS, fixes fertility or balances hormones, supplements or detoxes.
- Using the app's dates for contraception or conception timing.
